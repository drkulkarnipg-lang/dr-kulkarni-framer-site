02

ELDERLY & BEDRIDDEN PATIENT CARE

Elderly and bedridden patient assessment

Home assessment for elderly, frail, mobility-limited and bedridden patients can include new symptoms, hydration and nutrition concerns, mobility, medicines and caregiver concerns. Blood pressure and SpO2 (oxygen saturation) may be checked when appropriate, with review of post-discharge needs and advice on further care.

03

POST-HOSPITAL & POST-ICU CARE

Follow-up after hospital or ICU discharge

Review of discharge summaries, current prescriptions, symptoms and recovery after a hospital or ICU stay. Discuss monitoring requirements, follow-up appointments and concerns about the discharge plan. Keep reports and medicines ready. Home follow-up does not replace hospital or ICU care when those services are required.

04

RESPIRATORY & OXYGEN-RELATED HOME CARE

Respiratory assessment and home care

Assessment of breathlessness, cough, wheezing and asthma/COPD-related symptoms when suitable for a home visit. Care may include SpO2 assessment, nebulization-related care and review of existing oxygen therapy. Follow the prescribed plan; do not change oxygen settings or medicines without clinical advice.

Severe or rapidly worsening breathlessness, chest pain, altered consciousness or severe hypoxemia (very low blood oxygen) requires urgent emergency/hospital assessment. Do not wait for a home visit.

05

CHRONIC DISEASE FOLLOW-UP

Follow-up for established chronic illnesses

Clinically appropriate follow-up for hypertension, diabetes, chronic respiratory disease and other established chronic illnesses. Review symptoms, medicines, recent reports and home blood-pressure or glucose readings, where available. Treatment and follow-up are tailored to the patient’s condition; do not change prescribed medicines without clinical advice.

  • Palliative or supportive-care needs

Health Education

Practical information for patients and caregivers, from preparing for a home visit to understanding respiratory care. This general guidance supports conversations with your doctor; it is not an individual diagnosis or treatment plan.

Breathlessness & Respiratory Health

Note when breathing symptoms started and whether they are worsening. Severe or rapidly worsening breathlessness needs emergency assessment, not a delayed home visit.

Elderly Home Care

Keep a record of new symptoms, eating and drinking, mobility changes and caregiver concerns. Have current medicines and recent reports ready for review.

Oxygen & SpO2

SpO2 estimates blood oxygen saturation. Readings need clinical interpretation alongside symptoms. Follow the prescribed oxygen plan and keep equipment away from flames and smoking.

Asthma & COPD

Follow your prescribed inhaler and nebulization plan. Discuss wheezing, cough or changes in breathing with your clinician; do not change treatment on your own.

Post-Hospital Care

Keep discharge summaries, prescriptions and follow-up instructions together. Ask which symptoms to watch for, what monitoring is needed and when to seek hospital care.

Common Home-Care Questions

Before requesting a visit, share the patient’s age, main concern and location. Ask about clinical suitability, availability, fees and which records to keep ready.
